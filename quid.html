01

Context

Quid helps international students settle into the UK: a daily allowance that keeps money on track, a first-week checklist, an AI that knows UK student life, student deals and a community. I led the app design end to end, then designed and shipped the website.

02

The problem

685K international students arrive in the UK each year. Over half run out of money before term ends. A bank account can take 4 to 5 weeks, and the first week is a maze of BRP, SIM, GP and housing.

03

Research

How

UK university and government dataStudent forums and app store reviewsTeardown of budgeting and settling-in apps

What we learned

Insight 01

Money runs dry

Budgets plan by the month. Students spend by the day.

Insight 02

Banking is the bottleneck

Weeks without a UK account blocks everything else.

Insight 03

Asking feels like failing

Students search alone at 2 am rather than ask.

Insight 04

The lonely first month

When people need peers most, they have none.

Insight 05

Deals must be near

Savings only help if they're local and relevant.

05

Structure and direction

Information architecture

Every module and state mapped before any UI: auth, onboarding, home, money, AI, deals, community, profile.

Moodboards to a visual system

Five directions, from warm "Home Base" to Gen-Z "The Crew" and numbers-first "Control Room". Landed on lime on black, Google Sans and Inter.

06

The design

Onboarding

Phone code or Google, no passwords. Onboarding cut from 5 screens to 3.

Home, Money, Deals

One daily number, a first-week tracker, a live currency converter and deals from your checklist.

Key decisions

01

One number a day

A daily allowance replaces the budget sheet. Spend, and it updates.

02

No passwords, three screens

Sign up and log in became one Continue.

03

Think in two currencies

Every amount can show in pounds and the currency students think in.
